# Materials and Recipe Design

Use this note when creating harvest tables and crafting recipes for new monsters.

## Step 1 — Assign Monster Tier

| Tier | Typical CR | Typical Crafted Power |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | mundane+, consumables, minor magical traits |
| 2 | 5–8 | uncommon-equivalent |
| 3 | 9–12 | rare-equivalent |
| 4 | 13–16 | very rare-equivalent |
| 5 | 17+ | legendary-equivalent |

Boss monsters can count as one tier higher for one signature material without increasing every material they produce.

## Step 2 — Identify Material Families

Most monsters need 4–7 meaningful materials rather than a list of every anatomical component.

### Structural Materials
Used for frames, armor, blades, and reinforcement.

- bone
- horn
- tusk
- claw
- fang
- shell
- scale
- chitin

### Flexible Materials
Used for armor, straps, bows, clothing, movement traits.

- hide
- leather
- fur
- feather
- membrane
- tendon
- sinew

### Alchemical Materials
Used for consumables and elemental properties.

- blood
- venom
- bile
- mucus
- oil
- gland
- sac
- spores

### Arcane Materials
Used for signature magic properties.

- core
- crystal
- heart
- eye
- brain tissue
- planar residue
- soul fragment
- runic organ

## Step 3 — Give Materials Traits

Traits answer: **what can this material reasonably do?**

### Elemental Traits

- Fire
- Cold
- Lightning
- Acid
- Poison
- Thunder
- Radiant
- Necrotic
- Psychic
- Force

### Physical Traits

- Armored
- Razor
- Heavy
- Flexible
- Elastic
- Adhesive
- Buoyant
- Conductive
- Insulating
- Regenerative

### Behavioral Traits

- Predatory
- Ambush
- Pack
- Territorial
- Berserk
- Camouflaged
- Burrowing
- Aquatic
- Flying
- Climbing

### Magical Traits

- Arcane
- Divine
- Primal
- Planar
- Telepathic
- Illusory
- Temporal
- Gravitic
- Shadow
- Titanic

A material normally has **1–2 traits**. A signature Tier 4–5 catalyst may have 3.

## Step 4 — Build a Harvest Table

For each material, record:

- Material Name
- Anatomical Source
- Tier
- Traits
- Harvest DC
- Typical Units
- Stability
- Special Requirement

### Suggested DC by Material

| Material | Base DC |
|---|---:|
| Bone, teeth, large scales | 10 |
| Hide, horn, claw, membrane | 13 |
| Blood, venom, common gland | 13 |
| Delicate sac, eye, marrow | 16 |
| Magical core or rare gland | 19 |
| Elder/unstable catalyst | 22+ |

## Step 5 — Pick Signature Materials

Every major monster should have **one or two materials players get excited about**.

Examples:

- Inferno Sac
- Storm Heart
- Mirror Scale
- Void Eye
- Titan Marrow
- Frost Crown

A signature material should connect directly to the monster's most memorable feature.

## Step 6 — Connect Part Breaks to Materials

At least half of a boss monster's breakable parts should interact with harvesting.

Possible benefits:

- automatically recover the part
- reduce Harvest DC by 2
- add one unit
- unlock a rare material
- prevent the material from being ruined

Example:

> **Stormhorn:** If both horns are broken before the thunderbeast dies, the Storm Crown can be harvested automatically. Otherwise it requires a DC 19 harvest check.

## Recipe Formula

A simple permanent recipe uses:

**Base Item + Structural Material + Trait Material + optional Signature Catalyst**

Example:

> Longbow + wing bone + storm sinew + storm heart = lightning-themed monster bow.

## Recipe Complexity

### Tier I
- 2 material units
- one minor property

### Tier II
- 3 material units
- one significant property
- optional +1 enhancement if appropriate

### Tier III
- 4 material units
- one major property or two moderate properties
- often +1 or +2 enhancement

### Tier IV
- 5 material units
- two strong properties
- rare signature catalyst

### Tier V
- 6+ units
- unique legendary property package
- normally requires a specific legendary monster organ

## Property Budget

When designing items, use **Property Points** as a rough DM balancing tool.

| Property | Points |
|---|---:|
| Minor utility/cosmetic feature | 0.5 |
| +1 skill bonus or narrow advantage | 1 |
| +1d4 damage once/turn | 1 |
| Limited movement feature | 1 |
| 1/day 1st–2nd level spell-like effect | 1 |
| +1 weapon/armor bonus | 2 |
| +1d6 damage once/turn | 2 |
| Short-rest moderate active power | 2 |
| Conditional resistance | 2 |
| +2 weapon bonus | 3 |
| Permanent damage resistance | 3 |
| Strong reaction/defense feature | 3 |
| +3 weapon bonus | 4 |
| Powerful encounter-changing ability | 4+ |

Suggested budgets:

| Craft Tier | Point Budget |
|---|---:|
| I | 1 |
| II | 2–3 |
| III | 4–5 |
| IV | 6–7 |
| V | 8+ |

This is not a mathematical replacement for judgment. Compare the finished item with normal 5e items of similar rarity.

## Elemental Weapon Guidelines

Good ways to represent elemental monsters:

- extra elemental damage once per turn
- limited-use burst
- elemental damage conversion
- condition tied to repeated hits
- bonus effect against creatures vulnerable to that element

Avoid adding large extra damage to **every hit** on characters with Extra Attack unless the item tier supports it.

## Armor Guidelines

Good monster armor traits include:

- resistance
- temporary resistance
- reaction to reduce damage
- retaliation
- environmental immunity
- movement type
- advantage against one condition
- AC enhancement

Do not stack too many always-on defenses on one item.

## Monster Ability Conversion

When turning a monster ability into equipment:

### Reduce Scale
A 10d6 breath weapon might become 3d6 once per rest.

### Reduce Frequency
Recharge 5–6 might become once per short rest.

### Narrow the Area
A 60-foot cone might become a 15-foot cone.

### Add Attunement
Powerful signature traits should require attunement.

### Keep Identity
The item should still remind players of the monster.

## Recipe Families

A single monster can support an entire gear family.

Example:

### Emberjaw Set
- Emberjaw Blade — offensive fire weapon
- Emberjaw Guard — heat-resistant shield
- Emberjaw Mantle — fire protection armor
- Ember Bomb — consumable explosive
- Ember Oil — temporary fire coating

This encourages repeated hunts without requiring identical loot.

## Material Substitution

A recipe may accept a compatible substitute if:

- it has the required trait,
- it is at least the required tier,
- the anatomy/material can plausibly serve the same crafting role.

Using a substitute increases the crafting DC by **2** unless the recipe is designed to be flexible.

## Cross-Monster Crafting

Some of the best equipment should require materials from multiple creatures.

Example:

> **Tempest Fang** requires a razor fang from an apex predator, conductive scales from a lightning monster, and an arcane core from a storm elemental.

Cross-monster recipes create natural quest chains.

## Set Bonuses — Optional

If the party loves Monster Hunter-style armor sets, assign each monster armor set 3 pieces.

Suggested structure:

- **2 pieces:** small thematic passive benefit.
- **3 pieces:** stronger signature benefit.

Do not require a character to fill every armor slot if your 5e equipment model does not normally support separate slots. Treat "pieces" as named crafted accessories, armor upgrades, or set components.

## DM Checklist for a New Monster

- [ ] Assign monster tier
- [ ] Pick 4–7 materials
- [ ] Give each material 1–2 traits
- [ ] Set harvest DCs
- [ ] Choose 1–2 signature materials
- [ ] Create 2–4 breakable parts
- [ ] Connect breaks to harvest rewards
- [ ] Draft 3–5 recipes
- [ ] Compare crafted items to official rarity benchmarks
- [ ] Put the finished monster into [[Templates/Monster Hunt Template]]
