# Monster Parts and Part Breaks

Part breaking turns a boss fight into a tactical hunt. Hunters can deliberately damage wings, horns, tails, armor plates, claws, sacs, or other important anatomy.

## Designing Breakable Parts

A major monster should usually have **2–4 breakable parts**.

Good candidates:

- head or horns
- tail
- wings
- claws
- shell or armor plate
- elemental organ
- venom sac
- leg
- jaw or tusks

Each part needs four pieces of information:

1. **Targeting modifier** — how hard it is to hit.
2. **Break Threshold** — how much qualifying damage it can take.
3. **Break Effect** — what changes in combat.
4. **Harvest Result** — what material becomes available or safer to carve.

## Targeting a Part

A hunter declares the targeted part **before making the attack roll**.

Use the monster's normal AC, then apply a modifier:

| Part | AC Modifier |
|---|---:|
| Huge/easy target: torso plate, giant wing | +0 |
| Normal target: head, leg, large horn | +2 |
| Small/mobile target: claw, throat sac, eye ridge | +4 |
| Tiny/precise target | +6 |

If the attack misses because of the part modifier but would have hit the monster's normal AC, the attack **hits the monster but deals no Part Damage**. This keeps targeted attacks from becoming all-or-nothing.

## Part Damage

When a targeted attack hits, damage is dealt to the monster normally and also counts toward that part's **Break Threshold** if the damage type can reasonably harm it.

Do not create a second HP pool that absorbs damage. Part Damage is a tally only.

### Suggested Break Threshold

A part's threshold can be based on the monster's maximum hit points:

| Part Durability | Threshold |
|---|---:|
| Fragile | 10% max HP |
| Standard | 15% max HP |
| Tough | 20% max HP |
| Extremely tough | 25% max HP |

Round to a convenient multiple of 5.

Example: A 180 HP wyvern might have a wing threshold of 30 and horn threshold of 35.

## Damage Suitability

The DM may decide certain damage is especially good or bad for a part.

### Effective Damage
If a damage type is especially effective, count **1.5× damage** toward the Break Threshold, rounded down. The monster still takes normal damage unless another rule says otherwise.

Examples:

- bludgeoning against shells, horns, stone plates
- slashing against tails, tendons, membranes
- piercing against sacs, eyes, soft joints

### Ineffective Damage
If a damage type is poorly suited, count **half damage** toward the threshold.

Examples:

- piercing against thick shell
- slashing against stone armor
- fire against a heat-resistant flame organ

## Breaking a Part

When accumulated Part Damage equals the threshold, the part **Breaks**.

Immediately apply its Break Effect.

Common effects:

### Broken Wing
- flying speed reduced by 10–20 ft., or
- monster must land at the end of its turn, or
- first time each round it flies, it must succeed on a Dexterity save or descend.

### Broken Leg
- walking speed reduced by 10 ft.
- cannot take Dash action
- may lose a pounce or charge feature

### Broken Horn
- reduce spell/ability save DC by 1
- remove one charge or use of a signature feature
- prevent a magical shield from refreshing

### Broken Jaw
- bite damage reduced by one damage die
- grapple-on-bite disabled

### Broken Armor Plate
- AC reduced by 1 or 2
- reveal a vulnerable spot

### Ruptured Elemental Organ
- signature elemental attack deals one fewer damage die per size/tier
- recharge range worsens, such as Recharge 5–6 becoming Recharge 6

### Severed Tail
- remove tail attack
- place the severed tail on the battlefield as an immediately recoverable material

## Severing Parts

Some parts can be **Severed** rather than merely broken.

A severable part usually requires:

- the part to reach its Break Threshold, and
- at least half of its accumulated Part Damage to come from **slashing damage**.

When severed, the body part falls nearby. It cannot be ruined by what happens to the rest of the monster unless the environment destroys it.

## Critical Hits on Parts

A critical hit against a targeted part counts an additional amount of Part Damage equal to the attacker's proficiency bonus.

This bonus does not increase damage dealt to the monster.

## Called Shots and Spells

A spell can target a body part if:

- it uses an attack roll, or
- the spell's effect clearly strikes a specific location and the DM allows it.

Area effects normally damage the creature but **do not accumulate Part Damage** unless the area is tightly localized or the monster part occupies much of the effect.

## Part Break Rewards

Each broken part should provide at least one payoff:

- combat advantage
- guaranteed common material
- improved chance at rare material
- extra carve attempt
- access to a special recipe
- reduced monster ability

## Ruining Parts

A part can be ruined if the fiction strongly supports it.

Examples:

- incinerating a hide needed intact
- shattering a crystal organ with thunder damage
- dissolving scales in acid
- destroying a venom sac with massive piercing damage

Avoid gotchas. If a player's action risks destroying valuable material and their character would reasonably know that, **warn them before the roll**.

## DM Guidance

Part breaks should make fights **more interesting, not dramatically easier**. Avoid putting every major ability behind a single fragile part.

A strong boss might lose one attack when its tail is severed but gain a more aggressive close-range behavior in response.
