# Carving and Harvesting

Harvesting determines what useful materials survive after a monster is defeated.

The goal is to make carving meaningful without turning every carcass into an hour of bookkeeping.

## Harvest Sequence

After a monster is slain:

1. **Secure the Area** — combat and immediate hazards must be resolved.
2. **Assess the Carcass** — identify usable parts and damaged materials.
3. **Choose Harvest Targets** — hunters decide what they want first.
4. **Make Harvest Checks** — each target requires time and a check.
5. **Record Materials** — note quantity, quality, and special traits.
6. **Preserve Materials** — some parts spoil without treatment.

## Harvesting Tools

A basic **Hunter's Carving Kit** contains heavy knives, saws, hooks, forceps, scrapers, vials, wrapping cloth, and sharpening tools.

A creature proficient with one of the following can usually apply that proficiency when appropriate:

- Hunter's Carving Kit
- Survival
- Medicine
- Nature
- leatherworker's tools
- alchemist's supplies
- poisoner's kit

The DM chooses the most appropriate ability score, usually Dexterity, Wisdom, or Intelligence.

### No Proper Tools
Harvest checks made without suitable tools have **disadvantage**. Certain delicate organs cannot be harvested without containers or specialized equipment at all.

## Assessing the Carcass

A hunter may spend 1 minute examining the body and make an Intelligence (Nature) or Wisdom (Medicine) check.

| Monster Tier | Assessment DC |
|---|---:|
| Tier 1 | 10 |
| Tier 2 | 12 |
| Tier 3 | 14 |
| Tier 4 | 16 |
| Tier 5 | 18 |

On a success, reveal:

- all obvious harvestable parts,
- which parts were ruined,
- which parts are unstable or time-sensitive,
- one potentially rare material.

On a success by 5 or more, reveal all rare material opportunities on the carcass.

## Monster Tiers

Use whichever tier best matches the creature's threat.

| Tier | Typical CR | Material Quality |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Common |
| 2 | 5–8 | Refined |
| 3 | 9–12 | Superior |
| 4 | 13–16 | Masterwork |
| 5 | 17+ | Legendary |

The DM can move an unusual creature up or down one tier.

## Harvest Attempts

A carcass provides a limited number of **Harvest Attempts**. One attempt means one meaningful extraction, not one individual scale or tooth.

Suggested attempts:

| Creature Size | Base Attempts |
|---|---:|
| Tiny | 1 |
| Small | 2 |
| Medium | 3 |
| Large | 4 |
| Huge | 5 |
| Gargantuan | 6 |

### Boss Bonus
A major hunt target gains **+2 Harvest Attempts**.

### Broken Part Bonus
A body part intentionally broken or severed during combat may be collected **without spending a Harvest Attempt** if it is physically accessible and intact.

This rewards deliberate part-breaking.

## Harvest DC

Each material has a Harvest DC based on how difficult it is to extract intact.

| Difficulty | DC |
|---|---:|
| Obvious/tough material | 10 |
| Standard organ or hide | 13 |
| Delicate gland/crystal | 16 |
| Rare magical organ | 19 |
| Extremely unstable part | 22+ |

Modify as needed:

- part was deliberately broken cleanly: **-2 DC**
- ideal specialized tool: **-2 DC**
- carcass badly damaged: **+2 DC**
- part suffered an especially destructive damage type: **+2 to +5 DC**
- hunter learned preservation method during research: **advantage**

## Making a Harvest Check

A hunter chooses one material and makes the appropriate check.

### Success
Gain the listed material.

### Success by 5 or More
Choose one:

- gain **+1 unit** of the material,
- improve the material's quality by one step for crafting purposes,
- preserve it automatically if it would normally spoil.

### Failure
The Harvest Attempt is spent, but the material is not gained.

### Failure by 5 or More
The material is damaged or contaminated. That specific extraction cannot be attempted again unless the DM rules another portion exists.

### Natural 20
Gain the material and roll once on the **Rare Find** table.

## Rare Find

| d6 | Rare Find |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Pristine specimen: counts as 2 units of the harvested material |
| 2 | Rare catalyst tied to the monster's element |
| 3 | Trophy-quality part worth additional gold or reputation |
| 4 | Unusual organ that unlocks a recipe |
| 5 | Material with a secondary elemental trait |
| 6 | Apex specimen: material counts as one tier higher for one recipe |

## Harvest Time

Each Harvest Attempt takes time.

| Size | Time per Attempt |
|---|---:|
| Tiny–Small | 5 minutes |
| Medium | 10 minutes |
| Large | 15 minutes |
| Huge | 20 minutes |
| Gargantuan | 30 minutes |

A severed external part takes half the normal time, minimum 1 minute.

## The Freshness Clock

Certain materials begin degrading after death.

Unless the creature or environment suggests otherwise:

- **Stable materials** — bone, horn, scales, shell: no immediate timer.
- **Perishable materials** — hide, meat, blood: begin degrading after 1 hour.
- **Delicate organs** — glands, eyes, sacs: begin degrading after 30 minutes.
- **Volatile magical organs** — elemental cores, planar glands: may begin degrading after 10 minutes.

Once a material passes its safe window, Harvest checks for it have disadvantage. After twice the safe window, it is normally unusable without magical preservation.

## Preserving Materials

Common preservation methods include:

- salt and wrapping
- ice
- alchemical preservative
- sealed glass containers
- magical cold
- oil or resin
- drying/smoking
- suspension fluid

A hunter with an appropriate kit can preserve a harvested item with a DC 12 check unless the item specifies a harder method.

## Damage and Carcass Condition

The DM should track only meaningful damage to harvestable parts.

Examples:

- A fireball does not automatically destroy every scale.
- A disintegrated monster obviously leaves little to carve.
- Repeated acid damage may ruin hide.
- Massive lightning damage might overcharge or destroy a lightning gland.

Use common sense and warn players when they are about to destroy something they know they need.

## Group Harvesting

Multiple hunters can work at the same time on different parts.

A helper can assist one harvester if the helper is proficient in the relevant skill or tool. Normal 5e Help rules apply.

## Example Carcass

### Emberjaw Wyvern — Tier 2, Huge

Base Harvest Attempts: 5  
Boss Bonus: +2  
**Total: 7 attempts**

Possible materials:

| Material | DC | Units | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Emberjaw Scale | 10 | 1d3 | Stable |
| Wyvern Hide | 13 | 1 | Perishable |
| Flame Sac | 16 | 1 | Delicate; 30-minute window |
| Ember Crest | 13 | 1 | Automatic if crest was broken cleanly |
| Wing Membrane | 13 | 1 | Easier if wing was broken |
| Wyvern Marrow | 16 | 1 | Requires bone saw |
| Inferno Core | 19 | 1 | Rare; volatile; 10-minute window |

## What Counts as One Unit?

A **unit** is an abstract quantity large enough to matter in a recipe.

Examples:

- enough scales to reinforce one weapon component,
- one intact horn,
- one preserved gland,
- a usable section of hide,
- enough venom for a batch of poison,
- a bundle of monster bone.

Do not count individual scales unless the table enjoys that level of inventory detail.

## Harvesting Living or Captured Monsters

A captured monster can sometimes provide nonlethal materials:

- shed scales
- blood samples
- venom
- fur
- mucus
- feathers
- naturally shed horn

Extraction that would maim or kill a captive still requires appropriate consequences and should not be treated as a free infinite-material source.
