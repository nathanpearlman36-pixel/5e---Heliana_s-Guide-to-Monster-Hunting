# Crafting Equipment

Monster materials become weapons, armor, tools, ammunition, consumables, and specialized hunting gear.

The crafting system is built around three questions:

1. **What are you making?**
2. **What monster traits are being transferred into it?**
3. **How powerful should the finished item be?**

## Crafting Requirements

A recipe lists:

- **Item Base** — longsword, bow, shield, leather armor, potion, etc.
- **Material Tier** — minimum monster tier.
- **Required Materials** — number and type of material units.
- **Catalyst** — optional rare component that grants the item's signature property.
- **Gold Cost** — mundane fittings, fuel, reagents, labor, and finishing materials.
- **Crafting Time** — downtime required.
- **Crafting DC** — difficulty of safely integrating the monster material.
- **Required Proficiency** — smith's tools, leatherworker's tools, alchemist's supplies, etc.

## Equipment Tiers

Use these tiers when designing recipes.

| Craft Tier | Suggested Character Level | Typical Power |
|---|---:|---|
| I — Hunter | 1–4 | minor property or specialized mundane item |
| II — Veteran | 5–8 | uncommon-equivalent |
| III — Apex | 9–12 | rare-equivalent |
| IV — Master | 13–16 | very rare-equivalent |
| V — Legendary | 17+ | legendary-equivalent |

A campaign can require the crafter's settlement, smithy, or Hunter Rank to match the recipe tier.

## Basic Material Cost

A typical permanent item requires:

| Craft Tier | Material Units | Suggested Gold Cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| I | 2 | 50–150 gp |
| II | 3 | 250–500 gp |
| III | 4 | 1,000–2,500 gp |
| IV | 5 | 5,000–10,000 gp |
| V | 6+ | 20,000+ gp |

At least one material should come from a monster of the appropriate tier.

Consumables usually require **1 material unit** and 10–25% of the permanent item's gold cost.

## Crafting Time

| Craft Tier | Base Time |
|---|---|
| I | 1 day |
| II | 3 days |
| III | 1 week |
| IV | 2 weeks |
| V | 4+ weeks |

A fully staffed guild forge can shorten these times. Field crafting should normally be limited to ammunition, repairs, traps, and consumables.

## Crafting Check

At the end of crafting, make one check using an appropriate ability and tool proficiency.

Examples:

- Strength or Intelligence + smith's tools
- Dexterity + leatherworker's tools
- Intelligence + alchemist's supplies
- Wisdom + herbalism kit
- Intelligence + tinker's tools

Suggested DC:

| Craft Tier | DC |
|---|---:|
| I | 10 |
| II | 13 |
| III | 16 |
| IV | 19 |
| V | 22 |

### Success
The item is completed.

### Success by 5+
Choose one minor masterwork benefit approved by the DM:

- item weighs half as much,
- item cannot rust or rot normally,
- reduce future repair cost by half,
- add a cosmetic monster trait,
- increase resale value by 25%,
- add one noncombat utility feature.

### Failure
The item is not completed. Half of the gold cost is lost, but the monster materials survive and can be reused after another period equal to half the crafting time.

### Failure by 5+
One common material unit is also ruined.

A rare catalyst is only destroyed on a natural 1 unless the recipe specifically says it is volatile.

## Cooperative Crafting

One character is the **Lead Crafter**. Up to two proficient assistants may help.

Each assistant who works for at least half the crafting time grants one of the following:

- +1 to the final crafting check,
- reduce crafting time by 10%.

The same assistant cannot grant both.

## Building Weapons

A monster weapon starts with a normal weapon profile and gains properties based on its materials.

### Tier I Examples
Choose one modest property:

- deals +1 damage of an appropriate elemental type once per turn,
- advantage on one narrow hunting-related check,
- counts as magical against the species it was made from,
- can produce a harmless elemental/cosmetic effect,
- grants +1 to damage against one monster category once per turn.

### Tier II Examples
Choose one significant property:

- +1 weapon,
- +1d4 elemental damage once per turn,
- limited-use burst ability,
- improved critical effect against a studied quarry,
- returning or specialized movement property.

### Tier III Examples
Possible structure:

- +1 or +2 weapon plus a moderate monster trait,
- +1d6 elemental damage once per turn,
- 1/short-rest signature ability,
- conditional advantage tied to hunting behavior.

### Tier IV–V
High-tier items can combine enhancement bonuses and signature monster abilities, but avoid duplicating a boss's strongest action at full strength.

A player item should feel like a **piece of the monster**, not the entire monster stat block strapped to a sword.

## Building Armor

Armor materials commonly grant:

- resistance or limited protection from an element,
- bonuses against forced movement,
- climbing/swimming movement,
- camouflage,
- reaction defenses,
- retaliation damage,
- reduced environmental penalties.

### Resistance Guideline
Permanent damage resistance is powerful.

- Tier II: temporary resistance for 1 round, limited uses.
- Tier III: resistance under a condition or for limited duration.
- Tier IV+: permanent single-type resistance can be appropriate.

## Shields

Monster shields are ideal for:

- elemental reaction defenses,
- shell or plate traits,
- blocking cones or lines,
- bracing against charges,
- reflected damage.

## Ammunition

One material unit can generally create:

- 10 pieces of special ammunition, or
- 5 pieces of stronger magical ammunition.

Examples:

- venom arrows
- explosive bolts
- frostshot
- armor-piercing bone ammunition

Special ammunition is usually consumed on use.

## Consumables

Monster organs are excellent for:

- potions
- oils
- bombs
- antidotes
- resistance tonics
- poisons
- weapon coatings
- smoke or scent lures

### Consumable Crafting
A batch normally requires:

- 1 relevant material unit,
- appropriate kit proficiency,
- 1–8 hours depending on complexity,
- a check DC 2 lower than a permanent item of the same tier.

## Upgrading Existing Monster Gear

Instead of discarding a beloved weapon, a hunter can upgrade it.

To raise an item by one Craft Tier:

1. provide new materials equal to **the new tier + 1** units,
2. include at least one material from a monster of the new tier,
3. pay half the normal gold cost for an item of that tier,
4. complete half the normal crafting time,
5. make the new tier's Crafting Check.

On success, improve or replace one property to match the new tier.

This lets a weapon evolve alongside its hunter.

## Matching Materials to Item Parts

Recipes feel better when anatomy matters.

Examples:

- horn → weapon edge, focus, piercing component
- scale/shell → armor plating or shield
- hide/fur → light/medium armor, cloaks, grips
- tendon/sinew → bows, strings, flexible reinforcement
- bone → weapon frames, ammunition, handles
- gland/sac → elemental property
- venom → poison effect
- eye → perception, targeting, detection
- heart/core → high-tier catalyst
- wing membrane → mobility, gliding, lightweight gear
- claw/fang → cutting or piercing weapon property

## Attunement

Use normal 5e attunement rules.

As a guideline:

- Tier I items rarely require attunement.
- Tier II items require it only for stronger active powers.
- Tier III+ signature weapons and armor usually require attunement.

## Repairing Monster Equipment

If monster gear is badly damaged, repairs usually cost:

- 10% of original gold cost,
- 1 compatible material unit for Tier III+ items,
- one-quarter of original crafting time.

## Recipe Discovery

Recipes can be learned from:

- examining harvested materials,
- guild research,
- veteran smiths,
- ancient manuals,
- successfully breaking a rare body part,
- crafting experiments,
- completing contracts.

A recipe should tell players **what they need to hunt next**. That turns crafting into adventure fuel.

## Example Recipe

### Emberjaw Flameblade
*Tier II weapon recipe*

**Base:** any sword  
**Materials:** 2 Emberjaw Scales, 1 Flame Sac  
**Gold:** 350 gp  
**Time:** 3 days  
**Tools:** smith's tools  
**DC:** 13

**Finished Item:** +1 sword. Once on each of the wielder's turns when it hits, it can deal an additional 1d4 fire damage. After this extra fire damage is dealt three times, the property cannot be used again until the wielder finishes a short or long rest.

The exact item property can be adjusted to suit the campaign's power level.
