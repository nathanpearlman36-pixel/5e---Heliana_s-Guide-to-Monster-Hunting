# Hunt Procedure

Use this procedure whenever the party undertakes a formal monster hunt.

## 1. Accept the Contract

Every contract should identify:

- **Target:** what creature is being hunted, if known.
- **Threat Rating:** suggested party level or Hunter Rank.
- **Location:** known territory, last sighting, or suspected lair.
- **Objective:** slay, capture, drive off, recover a specimen, rescue survivors, or investigate.
- **Reward:** coin, reputation, access, materials, crafting services, or guild privileges.
- **Special Conditions:** time limit, preserve a body part, avoid collateral damage, protect an NPC, capture alive, etc.

If the target is unknown, the contract can begin as an investigation.

## 2. Research the Quarry

Before leaving, each character may pursue one useful line of research. Common approaches:

- Arcana — supernatural traits, magical organs, curses, planar origins.
- History — previous sightings, famous hunts, old weaknesses.
- Nature — ecology, diet, tracks, mating behavior, migration.
- Investigation — reports, maps, damaged equipment, bodies, physical clues.
- Medicine — anatomy, organs, venom, blood, weak points.
- Survival — territory, spoor, likely routes, nests.

On a successful research check, reveal one **Monster Insight** from [[02 - Tracking and Research]].

## 3. Prepare

After research, the party chooses supplies. Preparation should matter.

Examples:

- antidotes against poison
- cold-weather gear
- climbing kits
- traps
- bait
- elemental oils
- fireproof blankets
- water-breathing magic
- spare ranged weapons
- harvesting tools

When a preparation directly answers a discovered weakness or hazard, the DM may grant **advantage**, reduce a DC by 2, or negate one environmental complication.

## 4. Enter the Hunting Ground

Divide the region into **Hunt Zones**. A small hunt usually needs 3–5 zones; a major expedition may use 6–10.

Each zone can contain one or more of the following:

- tracks
- environmental hazard
- minor monster
- resource node
- survivor or witness
- lair sign
- territorial rival
- evidence of the target's condition or behavior

The party proceeds using the tracking rules in [[02 - Tracking and Research]].

## 5. Build the Trail

The party accumulates **Trail Points** by finding meaningful evidence.

Suggested requirement:

| Hunt Difficulty | Trail Points Needed |
|---|---:|
| Simple | 3 |
| Standard | 5 |
| Difficult | 7 |
| Legendary | 9 |

A successful tracking scene usually earns 1 Trail Point. Especially strong evidence may earn 2.

When the party reaches the threshold, they locate the monster, its lair, or its immediate trail.

## 6. Initial Encounter

Not every first encounter must be the final fight.

Possible first-contact outcomes:

- the monster ambushes the party
- the monster is feeding
- the monster is fighting another creature
- the monster flees after taking damage
- the party catches it asleep
- the monster is protecting young
- the party discovers it is wounded or diseased

A major quarry should feel like a creature with behavior, not a stationary boss room.

## 7. Combat and Part Breaking

During combat, hunters may target breakable body parts using [[03 - Monster Parts and Part Breaks]].

Breaking parts can:

- remove attacks
- reduce movement
- reduce AC
- weaken special abilities
- create vulnerabilities
- improve harvest yield
- unlock rare crafting materials

## 8. Retreat and Pursuit

When a major monster falls to roughly **one-third of its maximum hit points**, or when dramatically appropriate, it may attempt to retreat.

A retreating monster might:

- flee to its nest
- burrow
- fly to a perch
- dive underwater
- call lesser monsters
- trigger a chase

Do not automatically prevent pursuit with arbitrary immunity. Give the party a short chase, skill challenge, or tracking scene.

### Wounded Quarry
A wounded monster leaves easier signs. Tracking checks against a monster below half its maximum hit points are normally made with **advantage** unless terrain or magic conceals it.

## 9. Final Showdown

The lair or nest should add something new:

- environmental danger
- home-field advantage
- additional actions
- destructible terrain
- eggs or young
- stored prey
- elemental vents
- vertical terrain

A creature cornered in its lair may also gain a desperation ability or altered behavior.

## 10. Slay or Capture

A hunt normally ends when the target is slain, captured, driven off, or otherwise satisfies the contract.

### Capture Option
If the campaign uses capture hunts, a monster is **Capturable** when all of the following are true:

1. It is at or below 25% of its maximum hit points.
2. It is restrained, unconscious, incapacitated, or caught in a specialized trap.
3. A hunter spends an action applying an appropriate sedative, binding, or capture device.

The target makes a Constitution saving throw against **DC 8 + the hunter's proficiency bonus + the hunter's Wisdom or Intelligence modifier**. On a failure, it is captured. On a success, it resists and cannot be subjected to another capture attempt until the end of its next turn.

Bosses may have advantage on this save or require a specific contract item.

## 11. Harvest

After a successful slay, use [[04 - Carving and Harvesting]].

The condition of body parts matters. Parts broken intentionally are often easier to claim; parts destroyed by inappropriate damage may be ruined.

## 12. Rewards and Advancement

A completed hunt can award:

- contract gold
- guild reputation
- Hunter Rank progress
- access to higher-tier contracts
- crafting materials
- recipes or blueprints
- settlement upgrades
- titles and trophies

For a monster-hunting campaign, reduce random magic-item treasure and let crafted monster gear provide much of the party's equipment progression.
